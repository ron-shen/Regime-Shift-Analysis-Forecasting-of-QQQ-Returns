CONCEPT DRIFT DETECTION IN QQQ RETURN PREDICTION MODELS
Interview Preparation — Strong & Weak Points
============================================================

STRONG POINTS:
--------------
1. Covariate shift vs. concept drift distinction
   - Used KS test to rule out covariate shift
   - Logical inversion argument: good folds had MORE covariate shift than bad folds,
     breaking the assumed causal link

2. Open-to-open target justification
   - Execution-realistic reasoning: observe close, enter at next day's open
   - Avoids overnight risk from after-market execution

3. Leakage prevention
   - Understood exactly how sklearn Pipeline enforces fit-on-train-only
   - Scaler fit only on training window, applied to test window

4. ElasticNet choice
   - Connected model choice to data constraints (1000 samples, 40 features, 25:1 ratio)
   - Tree models overfitting was empirically observed

5. l1_ratio cap at 0.5
   - Empirically tested pure Lasso (l1_ratio=1): all coefficients went to zero
   - 5 out of 11 CV folds performed better with range 0-0.5 vs 1.0

6. Honest about limitations
   - Did not oversell weak out-of-sample results
   - Acknowledged thin profit factor and small test set

7. Conditional predictability reframe
   - Good folds (5/11) prove features CAN be predictive in specific regimes
   - Concept drift explains the regime-dependence of that predictability


WEAK POINTS:
------------
1. Fold count inconsistency
   - Correct answer: 11 CV folds + 3 held-out test folds = 14 total
   - "6 bad folds" refers to the 11 CV folds only

2. KS test understanding
   - Used it correctly but unclear on the mechanics
   - Study: how the KS statistic is calculated, what the null hypothesis is,
     and why p-value < 0.05 means distributions are different

3. No multiple testing correction
   - Tested 15 features at alpha=0.05 without Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg correction
   - Expected ~0.75 false positives per fold by chance alone

4. 39% phrasing on resume
   - Resume says "39% of key predictive features" — implies a feature-level statement
   - Actually: average of (flipped_features / 15) across bad CV folds
   - More precise phrasing: "on average, 39% of top features experienced correlation
     inversions across bad folds"

5. Never considered "no persistent alpha" challenge
   - If Lasso drives all coefficients to zero, features may not be predictive at all
   - Reframe: good folds are the counterfactual — features are conditionally predictive

6. Weak out-of-sample results
   - Only 3 test folds — insufficient statistical power
   - Drift filter does NOT improve profit factor out-of-sample
   - Only MDD decreases — primary optimization target did not generalize

7. Profit factor = 1.02
   - Immediately reframe: "This is a diagnostic project, not a live trading strategy"
   - Point: identifying WHY models fail (concept drift) is the contribution,
     not finding a profitable edge
